## This is my story

I was born in El Rincón de la Victoria, in Málaga, and if I have to pinpoint exactly where
it all started, I have to go almost to the beginning: to the age of two. Yes, two. My first
contact with anything electronic was not a computer or a phone, it was a game controller in
my hands, playing next to my father. That was my door into the digital world, and honestly
I did not let go of it until I was eighteen.

For all those years, video games were how I understood technology. I learned without
realising it: how to solve problems, how to get it wrong and try again, how to go looking
for information when something would not work. But when I turned eighteen something
changed. I started to see that video games, the way I was living them, were a waste of
time, and I decided to get serious. I enrolled in the ASIR higher vocational degree at
Cesur, and that is where cybersecurity appeared in my life for the first time. It caught my
attention, yes, but I saw it from a distance. One of those things you like but feel are not
for you — not yet.

## The moment I did not know where to go

I finished the degree and ran into one of those moments nobody tells you about in the
movies: I had been through a rough patch and I genuinely did not know what to do or which
way to go. I had no clear plan. And instead of standing still, I made a decision: I spent
four months in England learning English, which was one of my big weaknesses. I could not
communicate, and that was closing doors.

Those four months were much more than a language course. I met wonderful people, I learned
to get by in an environment that was not mine and, above all, I had time to think. A lot of
time. And in that time a question I had been circling for years finally surfaced: is
learning cybersecurity really worth it for me?

But of course, behind that question came a harder one: where do I even start? There were so
many options, so many branches, so many paths… And yet one of them called to me in a way it
does not call to everyone: the idea of being the person who legally breaks industries.
Someone who finds the cracks before the bad guys do, who helps make things safer, and who
can make a living from it. To me that was not just a job. It was something that made sense.

## From theory to the first finding

After the summer of 2026 I started the specialisation course at The Big School. There I
learned the foundations I needed, tried several areas and discovered pentesting. I liked it
in general, but one thing really hooked me: web pentesting. It was love at first sight.
There was something about understanding how an application works, how its parts talk to
each other and where it can fail that caught me completely.

Close to finishing the course I started looking into bug bounty programs on YesWeHack. And
within a week I ran into my first bug: an RCE. A *Remote Code Execution*. My first accepted
finding was a vulnerability that, on paper, takes many people years to find. I had been at
this for no time at all. After that I thought another one would come soon, but reality said
otherwise: it did not. A month went by. And then, a month later, I found another
vulnerability — another RCE.

It still strikes me as curious. My first two accepted vulnerabilities were something as
complicated as an RCE. I do not tell it as a medal, but as a lesson: in this world you do
not always win through experience, but through curiosity, through insisting, and through
looking where others do not. And also as a reminder that after the high comes the silence,
and you have to learn how to sit in it.

## What I am building now

Because of all this I decided to study seriously. Right now I am preparing for HackTheBox's
CWES and CPTS, two certifications that will force me to organise everything I have learned
the hard way and on my own.

On top of that I was already enrolled in Digitech's cybersecurity master's degree, which I
started at the end of October. There I am touching every branch alongside professionals
from the industry and learning an enormous amount. It is one of those places where you
realise how much you still have to learn and, instead of getting discouraged, it motivates
you.

And there is something I never want to lose: the passion, and the search for a job in Red
Team. That is my goal. I do not say it as a stock phrase, I say it because it is what gets
me up every day when I sit down to study.

## The wins that kept me going

It has not all been certifications and courses. Two things gave me the motivation I needed
at exactly the right moment.

The first: the rewards. Getting paid for a finding is not only about the money — it is
confirmation that the hours you are putting in are bearing fruit.

The second, and the most important to me: I was the only success story, out of more than
two hundred students, of The Big School's specialisation course. When they told me I could
not believe it. Not because I considered myself better than anyone, but because it was
proof that all that effort, all those nights, all those hours in front of the screen were
actually worth something.

## Thanks to those who held me up

And none of this would have been possible without my father and my mother. They have been
the fundamental pillars, and I mean that literally. They were there in the high moments,
when everything was bright and seemed to be going well, and also in the low ones, when I
needed motivation and could not find it anywhere else. A few years back I went through bad
patches, and they were there. This blog is a little bit theirs too.

## About the frog

The amphibious general presiding over this page is Kermit the Frog. I have always loved
him, and seeing him dressed as a general with that long-suffering face had a funny touch
that won me over. He stayed on as my logo, and these days some people recognise the frog
before they recognise the handle.

## My reality, no hype

I have been in cybersecurity for only four months. Four months. And even if it sometimes
looks like I have done a lot, I am aware that I am still starting out. I have a hard, fun
learning curve ahead of me, full of emotion and knowledge. But it is exactly the curve I
want to walk.

That is why this blog is going to be, above all, a writeup notebook. I do not want to write
from the pose of the expert who already knows everything, because I am not one. I want to
write from the real starting point: that of someone who tries, who fails, who spends a week
finding nothing and who one day runs into something he did not expect. Here I will mainly
publish my bug bounty writeups whenever the program allows it, plus notes, labs and
resources that help me along the way. I want the process to be visible exactly as it is:
the reconnaissance, the testing, the dead ends, what works, what does not, and what I wish
I had known earlier.

And I want this to be an honest site. If I publish a writeup about a lab machine, I will
say it is a lab. If I publish something related to a bug bounty program, I will respect its
rules, its scope and its disclosure policy. I will not publish vulnerability details
without permission, I will not break embargoes, and I will anonymise domains, personal data
and any sensitive information. Ethics is not decoration here: it is part of what I want to
learn and part of what I want to pass on.

I do not know exactly where I will be in five years. But I do know I want to keep learning,
keep writing writeups, keep looking for my place in Red Team and keep remembering where I
come from: a kid from El Rincón de la Victoria who started with a controller in his hands
at the age of two and who, twenty-one years later, is learning to find the cracks so that
others do not have to suffer them.

If you made it this far, thank you. This is only the beginning.
